B2B outbound benchmarks for 2026: reply, meeting and close rates

By Jamil Ahmed · Outbound · 01 Sep 2026

Everyone asks for benchmarks and almost nobody publishes usable ones, so here are the ranges we actually see across the outbound campaigns we run for B2B companies. A healthy cold email motion in 2026 lands a 2% to 6% reply rate, turns roughly half of positive replies into booked calls, and closes somewhere between 10% and 25% of the meetings that were properly qualified. If your numbers sit far below any of those, the useful question is not how to raise everything at once. It is which single stage is broken.

Reply rate: the number everyone stares at

Reply rate gets all the attention because it is the first number the tools show you. The ranges worth holding in your head: under 1% total replies means something upstream is broken, usually deliverability or a list built on lazy filters. Between 2% and 6% is a working campaign. Above that usually means a small, sharp segment and a message written for exactly one person, which is what you should be aiming at anyway.

One warning on the metric itself. Total reply rate includes the polite no and the angry no. The number that pays is positive reply rate, people expressing real interest. A campaign with 5% replies that are all unsubscribes is worse than one with 2% replies where half want a call.

Reply to meeting: where speed quietly wins

The least discussed stage is the gap between someone saying yes and a call actually landing on the calendar. Around half of positive replies should become meetings. Teams that reply within minutes book dramatically more than teams that reply the next day, because interest from a cold email is a spike, not a state. Reply fast, offer times instead of asking for them, and put the booking link one click away.

Most outbound funnels do not fail at the copy. They fail in the four hours between the reply and your response.

Meeting to close: the definition does the work

Close rates on outbound meetings vary more than any other stage because they depend on what you let count as a meeting. If any warm body on a call counts, your close rate will look terrible and your calendar will be full of noise. A qualified meeting has a definition: right profile, right authority, a real problem you solve. Held to that, closing 10% to 25% of outbound meetings is normal, and the top of that range comes from tightening the definition, not from better closing technique.

How to use these numbers

  • Audit one stage at a time, starting from the top. Deliverability and list quality explain most weak reply rates before copy ever enters the conversation.
  • Measure positive replies, not total replies. Interest is the metric, activity is the vanity version of it.
  • Track reply-to-meeting speed in minutes, not days. It is the cheapest improvement available in the whole funnel.
  • Write down your qualified-meeting definition and hold every campaign to it. Every number downstream depends on it.
